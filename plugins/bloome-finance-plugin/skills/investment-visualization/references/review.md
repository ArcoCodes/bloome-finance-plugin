# Visual Review

Review the rendered report, not just its source.

## Pass 1: Evidence and comparison

- Every plotted value maps to an exact source or a disclosed calculation.
- Observed, estimated, and scenario values are visibly distinct.
- Dates, forecast periods, currency, units, and accounting bases are comparable.
- Shared source origins are not presented as independent confirmation.
- The visual includes material challenge evidence or uncertainty.
- The selected proxy or listed company fairly represents the compared alternative.

Stop and repair the research when any item fails.

## Pass 2: Editorial clarity

- The title communicates one conclusion.
- The graphic answers a decision-relevant question.
- The main pattern is visible before the reader studies details.
- Direct labels remove unnecessary legend lookup.
- Annotations explain the decisive inflection, threshold, or outlier.
- The chart adds information that prose or a tiny table would not convey better.
- The sequence of visuals advances the report rather than repeating it.

Replace the visual when it has no clear editorial job.

## Pass 3: Craft and accessibility

- Important values are printed.
- Units and time basis are visible.
- Axes and scales are honest.
- Color is not the only carrier of meaning.
- Text remains readable on desktop and phone widths.
- Annotation leaders do not collide with marks or labels.
- The source line is legible and concise.
- The accessible description explains the conclusion and encoding.
- The graphic remains understandable in grayscale.

## Five-second test

Show the rendered screenshot without surrounding prose. After five seconds, the reviewer should identify:

1. what changed or differs;
2. which alternative or factor matters most;
3. whether the result is historical, forecast, or scenario-based.

## Thirty-second test

After thirty seconds, the reviewer should be able to explain:

1. why the pattern exists;
2. what evidence supports it;
3. what uncertainty could change it;
4. how it affects the investment decision.

## Final decision test

Compare the visuals with `decision.md` and the executive judgment:

- The same alternative wins for the same reason.
- Valuation uses the same basis.
- Probability is not visually conflated with payoff.
- No decisive caveat disappeared during visual simplification.

If the visual changes the apparent ranking without changing the underlying evidence, redesign it.
